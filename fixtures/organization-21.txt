Interviewer: Just so you know – today you’ll get plenty of questions! But no worries if you forget something along the way, since I’ll be asking a lot and I know it’s a lot of information. From what I’ve seen so far, your organization is very embedded in the local community. Could you first describe how it’s actually structured?
Interviewee: Of course. We’re a public institution consisting of several units – for culture, handicrafts, and tourism. These units operate independently, but they are interconnected and cooperate as a whole.
Interviewer: Aha, great, thank you. I wasn’t completely sure, so I wanted to understand better where we are. I also came across information that you have set goals, directions, collaborations… are you already part of any initiatives related to industrial symbiosis?
Interviewee: I can say that we are one of the key connectors in the municipality. We cooperate with various associations, other organizations, and quite intensively with the public utility company – especially in events, projects, and various local initiatives.
Interviewer: Excellent. Then you probably also experience both economic and environmental benefits from this collaboration?
Interviewee: Yes, definitely. Although I would say that often the engagement isn’t mutual. We frequently act as initiators, while others join only when they see their own direct benefit.
Interviewer: I see. I also heard that your handicrafts center functions as a kind of local hub – connecting artisans, sole proprietors, small processors…?
Interviewee: That’s true. They are included in our programs. But there’s still a lot of room for improvement, especially in how businesses – particularly those working with wood – manage their waste and how they could cooperate more circularly.
Interviewer: Where do you see the biggest challenges in this kind of networking?
Interviewee: It’s very difficult to cooperate if there’s no legal basis. Even those who would like to collaborate don’t know whom to turn to. They lack information and a supportive environment.
Interviewer: So, essentially, what’s missing is a meeting point – where people could connect and find concrete common ground?
Interviewee: Exactly. Often they have to help themselves – and many times they don’t even realize they could cooperate, or how.
Interviewer: Is your organization included in sustainability goals? I would guess you follow certain standards.
Interviewee: Yes, we are included. Our destination holds the Green Destination label. I’m also the green coordinator – both within the national green scheme and locally. I have to monitor the performance of the economy, municipal services, and municipal projects. More than 100 criteria, enormous amounts of documentation. We cover everything – from handling invasive plants to waste management and spatial planning.
Interviewer: That’s really impressive! I must admit, I’ve done quite a few interviews with companies that claim to be sustainability-oriented, but I haven’t heard this yet.
Interviewee: Perhaps the advantage is that tourism is part of international schemes. Here I do it myself, while elsewhere municipalities have an additional dedicated employee. With us, it’s part of my role.
Interviewer: Excellent, truly. Would you say the community in general is open to sustainable cooperation?
Interviewee: Hard to say. People are somewhat oversaturated with terms like “sustainable,” “green”… That’s why I try to communicate in a down-to-earth way – I explain that we’re not inventing anything new, but simply returning to practices we already knew. That approach seems more effective.
Interviewer: I completely understand. What about legislation? Is it respected, for example in waste management?
Interviewee: The municipality performs rather poorly. We’re average at best when it comes to waste separation. Within our institution, however, we really try to act responsibly. We organize workshops with waste materials, recycle clay, reuse wooden scraps from CNC cutting.
Interviewer: Why do you think these differences occur? Are the reasons systemic, cultural, economic?
Interviewee: A lot comes down to awareness. In cities, waste separation is worse – people bring garbage from elsewhere and leave it at collection points. In apartment blocks, oversight is weaker. Village settings have a clearer structure, and composting also helps.
Interviewer: Really interesting. And what about general awareness – information exchange, education? Is that present?
Interviewee: Changing habits is hard. Plastic is still too cheap. Systemic changes would be necessary – for example, restrictions on importing cheap plastics from China.
Interviewer: Does your organization exchange materials with others? Any collaboration with artisans or sole proprietors?
Interviewee: Partly, yes. Our store carries no plastic products, we communicate sustainability messages at events, in brochures, exhibitions. But in terms of actual material exchange, there’s still a lot of potential.
Interviewer: And human capital? Is knowledge shared across the organization?
Interviewee: I would say yes. We are really well connected. We raise awareness among colleagues and the public – through events, labels, and leading by example. Our products also speak for themselves.
Interviewer: What kind of collaborations with companies do you build? Are they mostly existing partnerships or do you establish new ones?
Interviewee: Both. We have ongoing collaborations, and we also connect with new ones – with destinations, museums, national tourism organizations. On multiple levels.
Interviewer: How do you build trust?
Interviewee: Quite a lot on a personal level. Personal contact is still very important here – it’s the foundation. Formalities help, but without a personal approach, it doesn’t work. Everything is based on trust.
Interviewer: Where do you see opportunities for improvement in establishing new networks?
Interviewee: More presentations on different levels – tourism, handicrafts, culture… Each unit has its own dynamic. And that’s precisely why it’s important to connect sectors in projects and build networks with partners from other environments as well.
Interviewer: Excellent. I’m really glad I met you and got an insight into your organization’s work. You’re truly exceptional.
Interviewee: Thank you as well. It was a pleasure.
