Interviewer:
Thank you very much for taking the time for this conversation. We are interested in your experiences and reflections on sustainable practices and how your organization is embedded in the broader system of industrial symbiosis. May we start with the basics?
Interviewee:
Of course, please go ahead.
Interviewer:
How would you describe your organization – what is your internal structure, how does the work take place?
Interviewee:
Our organization is hierarchically structured. The management board sets strategic guidelines and ensures compliance with legislation. Under it operate key departments – sales and marketing are responsible for product development and customer relations, the risk management department for financial stability, the legal department supervises business compliance, and the claims department, which takes care of clients, is also important. In addition, we also have a finance and IT department as well as support services such as human resources, internal audit, and PR. Recently, we have been increasingly focusing on sustainable practices and the circular economy, which is becoming part of our broader business strategy.
Interviewer:
Excellent, a very structured environment. Would your organization be open to cooperation with other companies within industrial symbiosis networks – that is, to share financial investments and results?
Interviewee:
In principle, we are open to such cooperation. We believe that connecting with other stakeholders can bring important economic and environmental benefits. Of course, factors such as financial feasibility, legal restrictions, and regulatory compliance must be taken into account. One of the major obstacles is aligning the interests of all participants – companies have different goals, resources, and willingness to cooperate. But if the model were sustainable and the benefits tangible, we would certainly consider joining such a network.
Interviewer:
Great, thank you. What about current and future sustainability orientations – how do you see them at the national and local levels?
Interviewee:
Slovenia is committed to the transition to a low-carbon society, reducing environmental impacts, efficient use of resources, and the development of a circular economy. At the local level, these goals are reflected in the development plans of municipalities. In our environment, some forms of sustainable cooperation already exist, mainly in the field of energy efficiency. Although we are not yet directly involved in these schemes, we are closely monitoring them.
Interviewer:
And your location in Slovenia – does it represent any advantages for you, for example, for sustainable initiatives?
Interviewee:
Yes, our location is business-wise favorable. The community is inclined toward sustainable practices – local institutions encourage the use of renewable resources, circular economy, energy efficiency. There are numerous joint projects where different companies connect.
Interviewer:
How do you assess the legislation regarding recycling and reuse – is the support good?
Interviewee:
The legislative framework is well developed and consistent with European guidelines. The regulations encourage separate waste collection, the use of secondary raw materials, and responsible environmental management. For our business, this mainly means adjustments in investments and the implementation of sustainability guidelines.
Interviewer:
What about the influence of economic factors – do they represent an obstacle or an incentive for sustainable projects?
Interviewee:
Both. Key are cost efficiency, long-term profitability, access to incentives, and a clear legislative framework. Much depends on the readiness of companies, as well as cooperation with local and state institutions.
Interviewer:
What is your assessment of how Slovenia is reducing emissions, water consumption, and the amount of waste?
Interviewee:
We see progress – the use of renewable resources, energy renovation of buildings, recycling, public transport are being promoted… But at the same time, we are facing high initial costs and complicated procedures. Nevertheless, the direction is correct and necessary.
Interviewer:
What about relations between the local community and companies that implement sustainable measures? Is there an exchange of knowledge?
Interviewee:
Yes, mainly through chambers of commerce, development agencies, and research institutions. There are support programs and initiatives that promote the transition to a circular economy.
Interviewer:
Do you have experience with material exchange – for example, that the waste of one company becomes a resource for another?
Interviewee:
We know of examples, also in our case. We participate in the exchange of waste materials or secondary raw materials, which are reused in other production processes.
Interviewer:
How would you describe the human capital needed for successful industrial symbiosis? Do we have enough knowledge?
Interviewee:
Technical, environmental, legislative, and financial knowledge is needed. These are present in larger cities – universities, development centers, specialized companies. But at the local level, there is a lack of networking, broader understanding, and knowledge about the transfer of materials, information, and processes between companies.
Interviewer:
Who are your key partners and how did the cooperation begin?
Interviewee:
We cooperate with companies in the field of recycling, IT technologies, and sustainable solutions. We started with joint projects and knowledge transfer. Today we cooperate in the implementation of sustainable practices and resource exchange.
Interviewer:
How important is trust in such connections?
Interviewee:
Very important. We build it with transparency, consistency, and respecting agreements.
Interviewer:
What is the general opinion about cooperation between companies?
Interviewee:
Formal cooperation is present, mainly with state support. Informal cooperation is often more effective but less systematized. There are many opportunities for improvement.
Interviewer:
And how would you assess the level of trust in symbiotic networks?
Interviewee:
Trust exists, but it is not yet strong enough. Business secrets, different understandings of benefits, and difficulties in aligning interests represent many obstacles.
Interviewer:
Do you have any concrete proposals for improving existing or new networks?
Interviewee:
I propose more awareness-raising, simplification of procedures, digital tools for resource exchange, and better connections between industry and the research sphere.
Interviewer:
Finally – do you believe that your organization could further improve its efficiency through participation in industrial symbiosis?
Interviewee:
Certainly. Cooperation between companies can bring cost reduction, better resource utilization, and strengthened sustainable operations. We see opportunities and are open to new synergies.
Interviewer:
Excellent. Thank you for everything you have shared – your insights will be of great help to our research. We appreciate your time and willingness to cooperate.
Interviewee:
With pleasure. Thank you as well.
Interviewee:
The municipality has quite a poor result. We are known as averagely successful in separation. Within our institution, however, we really try to act responsibly. We organize workshops with waste material, recycle clay, and reuse wood waste from CNC cuts.
Interviewer:
What do you think, why do such differences occur? Are the reasons systemic, cultural, economic?
Interviewee:
Much is a question of awareness. In cities, separation is worse – people bring garbage from other places, leave it on collection points. In apartment blocks there is weaker control. Village environments have a more transparent structure, composters also help.
Interviewer:
Really interesting. What about general awareness – exchange of information, education? Is that present?
Interviewee:
It is difficult to change habits. Plastic is still too cheap. Systemic changes would be needed – for example, restrictions on the import of cheap plastic from China.
Interviewer:
As an organization, do you exchange materials with others? Any cooperation with artisans, sole proprietors?
Interviewee:
Partly yes. Our shop has no plastic products, we communicate sustainability messages at events, brochures, exhibitions. But in the field of material exchange there is still much potential.
Interviewer:
What about human capital? Is knowledge being transferred throughout the entire organization?
Interviewee:
I would say yes. We are really well connected. We build awareness among colleagues and in the public – through events, labels, example. Our products also speak for themselves.
Interviewer:
What kinds of cooperation with companies are you building? Is it more about existing connections or are you establishing new ones?
Interviewee:
Both. We have existing cooperation, and at the same time we are also establishing new ones – with destinations, museums, national tourism organizations. At various levels.
Interviewer:
How do you build trust?
Interviewee:
Quite on a personal level. With us, there is still a lot of personal contact – that is the foundation. Formalities help, but without a personal approach it doesn’t work. Everything is based on trust.
Interviewer:
Where would you see opportunities for improvements in establishing new networks?
Interviewee:
More presentations at different levels – tourism, handicrafts, culture… Each unit has its own dynamics. And precisely because of that, it is important that in projects we connect different sectors, build networks also with partners from other environments.
Interviewer:
Excellent. I am very glad that I got to know you and gained insight into the functioning of your organization. You are truly exceptional.
Interviewee:
Thank you as well. It was a pleasure.
