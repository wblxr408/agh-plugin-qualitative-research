Organization: 25
Interviewer:
Thank you for taking the time for this conversation. Let’s start with the basics.
Interviewer:
How would you describe your organizational structure?
Interviewee:
ELVEZ has a classic functional organizational structure, which is typical for manufacturing companies with high requirements for quality and traceability. The key departments include R&D and technology, production, quality control, procurement, logistics and warehousing, sales and project management, and top management. Each of these has clearly defined tasks and follows the requirements of the automotive industry.
Interviewer:
So it is a highly specialized structure, oriented toward reliability and quality?
Interviewee:
Exactly. Traceability and immediate action are crucial.
Interviewer:
Would you be willing to be part of an industrial symbiosis network that shares inputs and results?
Interviewee:
In principle yes, but in our industry this is very limited. As a Tier 2 supplier, we are bound by our customers’ specifications. Every material change requires their approval. Industrial symbiosis often involves secondary materials, which for us are mostly incompatible with requirements.
Interviewer:
So the main constraints are related to safety, compliance, and approvals?
Interviewee:
Exactly. If conditions existed that would allow compliance with legislation and standards, we would be ready to cooperate.
Interviewer:
And what about your current and future sustainability goals?
Interviewee:
Currently we are focused on ISO 14001, waste reduction, and energy efficiency. At the local level, we are not involved in formal sustainability partnerships, but there is potential for the future if conditions allow.
Interviewer:
Which regulations do you follow in this regard?
Interviewee:
National environmental law requirements, ISO standards, and of course the requirements of the automotive industry.
Interviewer:
What advantages does your location in Slovenia bring?
Interviewee:
We are in the heart of Europe, close to major automotive markets. We have good infrastructure and access to EU funds. The main advantages are logistics, qualified workforce, and support for innovation.
Interviewer:
How do you assess the community’s support for sustainability?
Interviewee:
It exists, but it must make sense technically, economically, and legally.
Interviewer:
What is your view on legislation regarding recycling and secondary raw materials?
Interviewee:
The framework is good, but not sufficiently adapted to highly regulated industries. It does not differentiate between sectors, although it should. We cannot simply change materials without customer approval.
Interviewer:
Which factors do you see as key for the success of sustainability projects?
Interviewee:
The main ones are economic – ROI, costs, market position. Also environmental – regulation, pressures. It is interconnected, because a project succeeds if it makes sense economically and environmentally.
Interviewer:
How do you assess Slovenia’s efforts to reduce emissions, water consumption, and waste volume?
Interviewee:
We have strategies and laws, but they do not translate well to the local level. We separate and report waste. For water and CO2, there are no concrete local incentives.
Interviewer:
How does Slovenia, in your opinion, treat companies that follow Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)?
Interviewee:
We formally support the goals, but practical support is weak. Most exchanges happen at the academic level. Companies would need more concrete platforms and operational networks.
Interviewer:
Does product or material exchange happen in practice between companies?
Interviewee:
Rarely. There is no transparency in flows, no legislative incentives, and no safety mechanisms for using secondary materials.
Interviewer:
What kind of human capital is needed for successful industrial symbiosis?
Interviewee:
Systems thinking, technical skills, project management, legal knowledge, digital skills. This exists partly, but not in SMEs. There is a lack of operational support and motivation to develop these skills.
Interviewer:
Who are your key partners and how did cooperation begin?
Interviewee:
Tier 1 customers, material suppliers, laboratories. Cooperation is based on quality and reliability. Sustainability was added later – through ISO 14001.
Interviewer:
How important is trust in these relations?
Interviewee:
Crucial. We build it with 0 ppm deliveries, certificates, fast responsiveness, and transparency.
Interviewer:
How do you assess formal and informal cooperation between companies in Slovenia?
Interviewee:
Formal cooperation works within projects, informal is mainly present among SMEs. We need more systemic support.
Interviewer:
What is the level of trust among stakeholders in symbiotic networks?
Interviewee:
Low to medium. Reasons are fear of disclosing information, lack of platforms, and lack of safety mechanisms.
Interviewer:
Do you have proposals for improving industrial symbiosis?
Interviewee:
Digital platforms for resource sharing,
Financial incentives,
Standardization,
Education,
Pilot projects.
Interviewer:
Do you think your company can still improve ecological efficiency?
Interviewee:
Yes, especially in rework processes, energy, reuse of materials. But only if conditions of safety, traceability, and economic viability are met.
Interviewer:
Thank you very much for your detailed answers – your experience offers a very valuable insight into the limitations and potentials of industrial symbiosis in highly regulated environments such as the automotive industry.
