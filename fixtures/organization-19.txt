Organization: 19
Interviewer: To start – how is your organization structured? How does work function internally?
Interviewee: The structure is based on teamwork. Department heads coordinate, each covering their own field, but we are interconnected. The director leads the management side, but ultimately the mayor has the final say.
Interviewer: Thank you. Would your organization be ready to join an industrial symbiosis network, where companies share costs and benefits?
Interviewee: Yes. If the approach is sensible and clearly defined, we would definitely support participation.
Interviewer: What do you see as the biggest obstacle?
Interviewee: The main difficulty is mutual understanding – how the different parties perceive the essence. Public administration and business often have very different perspectives.
Interviewer: Understood. What are your current or future sustainability goals – for example in your municipality or region?
Interviewee: There is cooperation between local government, farms, and tourism. For instance, the Brda Winery works together with individual farmers – its members.
Interviewer: Are you or your organization personally part of such an initiative?
Interviewee: Not at the moment.
Interviewer: What advantages does your location in Slovenia provide?
Interviewee: Proximity to nature, local recognition, accessibility. The community is inclusive, though sometimes I’d wish for more systemic support for these initiatives.
Interviewer: How do you view the legislation on recycling and reuse?
Interviewee: The framework is fairly clear, but the problem is, as often, in implementation. Too much bureaucracy and a lack of clear pathways for smaller projects.
Interviewer: What are the key factors for successful sustainability projects?
Interviewee: A combination of environmental and economic aspects. Much also depends on vision and willingness to cooperate.
Interviewer: How do you see Slovenia’s efforts to reduce emissions, water use, and waste?
Interviewee: There are some efforts, but much more could be done – in a more strategic and systemic way.
Interviewer: What about cooperation with companies pursuing sustainability goals – do you exchange knowledge, information?
Interviewee: Sometimes, yes, but it’s not systematic. It’s often left to individual initiative.
Interviewer: Are there also examples of material exchanges between companies?
Interviewee: Yes. In forestry, for instance – excess growth or worn-out wooden materials can be processed into fuel. Pine needles are used to make soft running tracks – “Holmer tracks.” Returning natural materials to nature is an interesting concept.
Interviewer: Very interesting. What about human capital – which competencies are key for successful industrial symbiosis?
Interviewee: The greatest capital is understanding others, knowledge-sharing, and minimizing envy and jealousy. Unfortunately, I can’t say this capital is sufficiently present in practice in Slovenia.
Interviewer: Who are your main partners?
Interviewee: Cooperation started back when I was a sole proprietor. I built long-term partnerships based on trust. Today, that continues in my current role. It’s mostly cooperation with small businesses and services.
Interviewer: How do you build and maintain trust with partners?
Interviewee: Through honesty, punctuality, and openness. I think that still counts the most.
Interviewer: What’s your general opinion on cooperation between companies in Slovenia?
Interviewee: The economy here is quite developed and offers many opportunities for cooperation. The possibilities are there – you just need to know how to find them.
Interviewer: How would you assess the level of trust in existing networks that exchange natural resources?
Interviewee: I’d say it’s positive.
Interviewer: Do you have suggestions for improving industrial symbiosis networks?
Interviewee: Better conditions for beginners, more promotion of professions, and greater encouragement of cooperation – especially among small businesses.
Interviewer: Finally – could your organization further improve efficiency?
Interviewee: Certainly. There’s always room for improvements and savings.
Interviewer: Excellent, thank you very much for your time and sincere answers. We greatly appreciate your contribution to this research.
Interviewee: Thank you, with pleasure.
