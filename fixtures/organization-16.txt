Organization: 16
Interviewer: First of all, thank you for taking the time for this conversation. To begin – could you please describe the organizational structure of your company?
Interviewee: Of course. It’s a part-time sole proprietorship, where I work alone. Occasionally, for larger projects, family members help me.
Interviewer: Thank you. Would you be open to participating in an industrial symbiosis network, where stakeholders share financial investments and results?
Interviewee: My activities are mainly business consulting, education, and sports activities, so I don’t generate material flows typical of industrial symbiosis. But if my services could be useful to other members of such a network, I’d be willing to participate.
Interviewer: What obstacles do you see in such a network?
Interviewee: Perhaps challenges would arise in environments where companies don’t want to reveal their competitive advantages. That could hinder open cooperation.
Interviewer: Understood. Are you currently part of any sustainable business partnerships?
Interviewee: No, I’m not. I work independently and am not involved in such collaborations. I’m also not very familiar with the relevant legislation.
Interviewer: How do you assess the current state of sustainable cooperation in your local environment?
Interviewee: In the environment where I mostly work, there aren’t many noticeable sustainable initiatives. Maybe that’s linked to the nature of my work, which is far removed from production activities, where such practices are more common.
Interviewer: What’s your view of the legislative framework for recycling and reuse?
Interviewee: I don’t know enough about the topic to comment. It doesn’t directly affect my business.
Interviewer: Which factors do you think are key for the successful implementation of sustainability projects?
Interviewee: Hard to say. These areas aren’t close enough to me to have a firm opinion.
Interviewer: What about your city’s or region’s efforts to reduce emissions and waste?
Interviewee: There’s visible progress – for example, bike-sharing systems, EV charging stations, and well-organized cycling infrastructure. Waste collection and separation are also well arranged.
Interviewer: Thank you, very concrete. Could you assess the cooperation of companies with the UN Sustainable Development Goals in your area?
Interviewee: Unfortunately not. I’m not directly involved in such initiatives and therefore can’t really judge.
Interviewer: The same applies to the exchange of material flows between companies?
Interviewee: Yes, I don’t know these practices, but I believe production companies have them more established.
Interviewer: How would you assess the importance of human capital for the success of symbiosis among companies?
Interviewee: It’s crucial. Skilled people who can think broadly and look for synergies are needed. If they’re not available locally, you can find them elsewhere in Slovenia – company culture and leadership are equally important.
Interviewer: Who do you usually collaborate with in your activities?
Interviewee: Most often with companies working in IT development and other service activities – education, consulting, etc. In sports, I collaborate with other trainers.
Interviewer: What role does trust play in these collaborations?
Interviewee: A very big one. If someone proves unreliable, cooperation ends quickly. Trust has to be mutual and based on keeping agreements.
Interviewer: How do you assess formal vs. informal cooperation among companies in your field?
Interviewee: Very good. Cooperation takes place both formally and informally. Companies strive to improve business performance, and that encourages collaboration.
Interviewer: Do you have an opinion on the level of trust in symbiotic networks?
Interviewee: Unfortunately not, because I don’t work in that area.
Interviewer: Do you have any suggestions for improving these networks?
Interviewee: Hard to say – without direct experience it’s difficult to provide relevant suggestions.
Interviewer: Could your company benefit from participating in such a network?
Interviewee: Not directly, since I’m not in production. But perhaps some partnership opportunities on another level could emerge.
Interviewer: Thank you very much for your answers and your time.
Interviewee: You’re welcome, thank you as well.
