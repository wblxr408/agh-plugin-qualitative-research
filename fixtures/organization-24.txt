Organization: 24
Interviewer:
So, we have started recording. Well, my first question is: could you briefly describe the organizational structure of your company?
Interviewee:
Our organizational structure is as follows: I am the director, and in addition I also take care of part of marketing, sales, and also communication – I actually deal with suppliers. Then there is Urban, he is basically the co-owner and product director, and he takes care of production. We also have, as you saw, one full-time employee, and then part-time, or rather, we have a partner who is a programmer, who helps us with development.
Then there is a lady who takes care of logistics – outsourced. And we also have one person specifically for Amazon, who takes care of sales on Amazon and helps. Then we also have another one – Matej – he has his own company, because he did not join ours formally. He is not employed, not even part-time; he helps, let’s say, about ten hours a week. He helps with marketing, mostly Facebook and such things, and Google Advertising.
That is the structure for now, only in production at the moment, actually, only one working with us and Urban – not that they are actually working in production, no, but we are thinking together with Katapult to later outsource the production in order to accelerate things. They also have students, and basically their employees, and they can outsource them into production, or they can also be in training. That is the structure.
Interviewer:
And what is your business about?
Interviewee:
We deal with the development of products for the maximization of human potential. The first product is Home on the Door 5, this token for overcoming phone addiction. That is our first product.
We already have some other products, but currently we are in the phase with this first product, we launched it. Now we are in the launch and testing phase on the market.
Then, that’s what we are dealing with. We also have a sort of bootstrapping side of the company, where we do work for other clients – for example, making websites, renovation of operational digital processes, and on the other side, development of embedded systems and connections.
Interviewer:
How long has your company existed now?
Interviewee:
Now we have existed since 13 February 2024. The idea lived with us for about six years before, we did some work on it, but then we formally established the company.
Interviewer:
If we move to the topic of this interview, which is industrial symbiosis. Earlier I explained what it means. My question is: are you already participating in this, or if not, would you be willing, do you see potential here?
Interviewee:
Our product is very simple, it only has three components – if you count in the bottle as well, then four, with the sticker it is five, with the packaging six. But the product itself has three main components. Cork – that is the upper part, then the NFC chip, and then wood below, which is basically a veneer board.
Now, we would be interested in this, maybe in the sense that some of these materials could come from waste sources. That way optimization could be done, also cost-wise. But currently, for cork, for example, we don’t know if it would be possible to take cork waste from somewhere, because our supplier, the one who makes it for us, is in Romania, and he has it under control there.
For wood maybe – if there was some larger company that cuts wood and has waste boards left over, this could be an option. For the NFC chips – they come from China, I don’t know if waste exists in this sense there, probably not. Packaging, as I understand, is already almost 100% recycled.
Interviewer:
So basically, from what I hear, you already use fairly sustainable materials, right?
Interviewee:
Yes. Cork is entirely sustainable, because only the bark is peeled, the tree itself is not cut down. Wood is wood, biodegradable in the end. As for the rest, about 95% is environmentally friendly. Only the NFC chip with adhesives is an exception.
Interviewer:
Okay. So what about here, in our local environment – in Slovenia – do you perceive that such sustainable processes, such as industrial symbiosis between companies, already exist?
Interviewee:
Yes. There are some examples. I don’t recall the company names exactly, but I know of two companies cooperating in this way. One company produces aluminum products, and the other takes their aluminum waste and uses it to make its own products. So this is happening locally, in a nearby town.
We might still be too small to be part of this yet, but I can imagine it. I also heard that Revoz (car manufacturer) gives some of its waste material to another company for reuse.
Interviewer:
Do you know if regulations exist on this topic, or are you not there yet as a company – for example, regulations on sustainability, quality control, etc.?
Interviewee:
Honestly, I haven’t really studied the regulations yet. I know there are some rules regarding packaging – I will probably have to look into that more in the future. One thing I have noticed is with logistics providers like GLS, DHL, DPD: when you sign a contract with them, you see there is an environmental fee on the invoice, a few cents per shipment, for CO2 costs. But other than that, I am not really familiar with special regulations.
Interviewer:
Okay. What do you see as the advantages of your location (Podbreznik / Novo mesto), also for sustainable cooperation?
Interviewee:
I think there are advantages. Unlike Ljubljana, where everything is too centralized, Novo mesto is well positioned – between Zagreb and Ljubljana, about 45 minutes each way on the highway. That is good. It is also a strong industrial area – Adria Mobil and other large companies are here.
It would be even better if the highway expansion eastwards happened, to connect more quickly with other towns. Then it would be an ideal location.
Interviewer:
And locally, do you get faster information, support, networking?
Interviewee:
Yes, for sure. For example, Rudolfovo is strong here, more research-oriented. Other small towns have development agencies, which help with tenders and starting projects. There are at least 20 entrepreneurs here with whom I am already connected.
Interviewer:
Do you generate a lot of waste in your production?
Interviewee:
Actually no. For our current product, the waste is minimal – cork waste is pressed back, wood is optimized so that almost nothing is left over. We designed it that way intentionally, both for cost and environmental reasons. For assembly, we just glue and program the NFC chip, then pack.
So in our own process, there is almost no waste.
Interviewer:
What factors do you think are most important for successful sustainable projects?
Interviewee:
As a startup, we look first at costs and finances. But sustainability is important – cork is both sustainable and affordable, and people like it. Even though in the long term plastic might be cheaper, we stick with cork because it is natural, feels good, and customers prefer it.
So yes, finances are the first priority, but we also consider user perception and environmental benefits.
Interviewer:
And your opinion on Slovenia’s effort to reduce CO2, water use, and waste?
Interviewee:
I think a lot is being done, especially through the EU. Personally, air quality worries me the most – it affects health directly. Living in a wooded area, CO2 and climate issues feel less immediate. But bad air – particulates – that is something you feel every day.
Interviewer:
And do you include sustainability measures in your business?
Interviewee:
I would say yes – our product itself is sustainable, mainly made of cork and wood. Except for the NFC chip, it could almost be composted. But we don’t yet have a systematic sustainability strategy.
Interviewer:
And what about incentives – do you see tenders or financial support opportunities?
Interviewee:
Yes, I get the SPIRIT newsletter every week. There are many tenders for sustainability, circular economy, etc. But honestly, most are for large companies with hundreds of employees. For us as a small startup, they don’t fit. We would need micro-grants, smaller funding opportunities.
Interviewer:
What about human capital – what kind of person should lead sustainability in a company?
Interviewee:
Someone in a higher decision-making position, aware, educated in the topic, with the power to make decisions. They must also be able to network and connect with other companies. Otherwise, sustainability will always be blocked by finance managers who only see costs.
Interviewer:
And what about trust in partnerships and networks?
Interviewee:
Trust is crucial. Without it, things don’t move. Contracts are important as a safeguard, but a handshake and mutual understanding save time. I once got burned with a foreign partner, so now I always make sure to meet partners at least online if not in person.
Interviewer:
What could improve industrial symbiosis in Slovenia?
Interviewee:
More events, networking opportunities, where directors meet and see the value of cooperation. And of course, financial incentives – every company appreciates them.
Interviewer:
And finally – where do you see potential in your company to improve efficiency through industrial symbiosis?
Interviewee:
With our current product, maybe using wood waste more efficiently, or replacing wood with recycled plastic boards. For NFC chips, sourcing from Europe instead of China. And later, when we scale, shared production facilities with Katapult will be important.
Interviewer:
Excellent, thank you.
