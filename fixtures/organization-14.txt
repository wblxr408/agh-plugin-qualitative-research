Organization: 14
Interviewee:
Our organization is structured for efficient implementation of solar power plant projects – from household systems to large free-standing installations. We have: a general director for strategic development, a technical director for technical solutions,a financial officer overseeing costs, a commercial officer managing client relations, a legal officer for contracts and legal matters, two installation teams, and a maintenance department.
Interviewer:
Are you open to participating in an industrial symbiosis network, sharing financial contributions and results?
Interviewee:
In principle yes – we see both economic and environmental benefits, such as resource optimization. The main challenge, however, is the different business goals of partners. Without clear rules and equal terms for all, we would not engage in such cooperation.
Interviewer:
What are your sustainability goals? Are you part of sustainability networks?
Interviewee:
Slovenia aims for climate neutrality by 2050. Locally, we apply to municipal tenders for solar plants on public buildings. We are not yet part of a larger network, but we are considering involvement in recycling solar plant components. We comply with legislation (Environmental Protection Act – ZVO, Renewable Energy Sources Act – ZRVE, Energy Act) and EU guidelines (Green Deal).
Interviewer:
What are the advantages of your location, and how do you assess community support?
Interviewee:
We have good infrastructure, but finding skilled labor is difficult. The rapid growth in demand for solar plants has overburdened the electricity grid, making connections harder to obtain. Between companies, we notice a lot of competitiveness and little openness.
Interviewer:
How do you assess legislation regarding recycling and reuse?
Interviewee:
Legislation is still unclear when it comes to waste from solar plants at the end of their life cycle. Waste separation and recycling are encouraged, but the guidelines are complex, procedures lengthy, and specific incentives are lacking.
Interviewer:
Which factors influence the success of sustainability projects?
Interviewee:
Financial accessibility – subsidies, loans. There are also challenges with spatial planning and lengthy administrative procedures. More flexible legislation is needed.
Interviewer:
What’s your opinion of state efforts to reduce emissions and waste?
Interviewee:
There are some measures – e.g. subsidies for solar plants through Borzen, improved waste collection infrastructure. But procedures are complicated and local measures vary by municipality.
Interviewer:
What about the relationship between the state and companies on sustainability?
Interviewee:
There are initiatives (conferences, workshops), but connections are not systematic enough. Collaboration is too project-based, and there is no consistent support framework for companies.
Interviewer:
Do you see examples of circular use of materials in Slovenia?
Interviewee:
Yes – cement factories use tires for energy, steel plants use scrap steel, biogas plants use organic waste. But all of this is based on individual company initiatives, not on a national strategy.
Interviewer:
What human capital is needed for successful symbiosis?
Interviewee:
Leadership vision, technical knowledge, economic analysis, coordination between companies and the public sector. We have skilled people, but not enough networking. More education and coordinated strategies are needed.
Interviewer:
Who are your key partners, and is there any resource exchange?
Interviewee:
We cooperate with companies from renewable energy, construction, and recycling. Partnerships are mostly built around shared sustainability interests. Exchanges are more about knowledge and solutions, not so much about materials.
Interviewer:
How important is trust in cooperation?
Interviewee:
Very important. Trust is built through consistency, open communication, and meeting deadlines. But it is a long-term process.
Interviewer:
What is your view on formal vs. informal cooperation?
Interviewee:
We rely mainly on formal cooperation. Informal approaches can be useful, but our experience shows contracts are more reliable, especially for complex projects.
Interviewer:
How would you rate trust levels among stakeholders?
Interviewee:
Trust exists where there have been positive past experiences. But additional mechanisms are needed – such as clear agreements and legal certainty.
Interviewer:
Do you have suggestions for improving symbiotic networks?
Interviewee:
Simplify legislation. Provide incentives for companies. Organize more training and workshops on the benefits of industrial symbiosis. Create platforms for connecting companies.
Interviewer:
Could your company improve efficiency further?
Interviewee:
Yes – through partnerships, shared infrastructure, digitalization, and more efficient use of resources. This would strengthen both competitiveness and business stability.
