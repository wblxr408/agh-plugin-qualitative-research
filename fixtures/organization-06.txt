Organization: 6
Interviewer:
Thank you for being with us today. To start – could you briefly describe your organizational structure?
Interviewee:
Our company doesn’t have a fixed organizational structure. We allocate employees on the go, depending on current needs. We adapt to projects.
Interviewer:
And what about your openness to participating in industrial symbiosis networks – would that interest you?
Interviewee:
Yes, we would be willing to cooperate – but of course, a lot depends on the conditions. If they are clear and fair, why not?
Interviewer:
What do you see as potential difficulties?
Interviewee:
For example, the distribution of financial contributions could be a problem. That can quickly become a sensitive issue among stakeholders.
Interviewer:
Are you involved in any sustainability partnerships or networks at the local or national level?
Interviewee:
No. To be honest – I don’t really know the situation in this area. We’ve never really delved into it.
Interviewer:
What about current support from the community or the state – do you feel any support for the sustainable development of micro-enterprises?
Interviewee:
In my opinion, this is not promoted enough. Personally, as an entrepreneur, nothing concrete has ever been presented to me. I would welcome if the state really took care of easier development – especially for us, micro-enterprises.
Interviewer:
How do you view legislation regarding recycling?
Interviewee:
It’s going in the right direction. People are increasingly aware of how important recycling is. For us, it has a positive effect – we separate waste, we’re organized. But the higher costs of hazardous waste disposal really surprise me – this leads to improper handling and illegal dumping sites.
Interviewer:
Which factors do you think influence the success of sustainability projects?
Interviewee:
Good infrastructure, organized municipal support, sensible business policy. And also the balance between labor costs and how much the company generates.
Interviewer:
As a citizen, how do you see efforts to reduce emissions, water use, and waste?
Interviewee:
Ljubljana is making efforts – electric buses, the “Kavalir” transport, better conditions for e-taxis. I think water consumption will decrease with higher prices – the more expensive it gets, the less we’ll use. The biggest problem is waste – food delivery has exploded, and there is too much packaging. My vision is a return to glass – glass instead of Tetra Paks.
Interviewer:
How do you assess the connections between companies and the UN Sustainable Development Goals?
Interviewee:
I would mention, for example, the goal of clean energy. This is a big problem for me. Electricity is getting more expensive. I believe we need a new nuclear power plant.
Interviewer:
Do you ever engage in material or resource exchanges with other companies?
Interviewee:
No. And I also have the feeling that as an entrepreneur here, you are quite on your own. When things are going well, everyone joins for promotion. But when things go badly – then no one is around.
Interviewer:
What is your opinion on human capital and its influence on symbiosis?
Interviewee:
Our education system is excellent. It prepares people well for work. But when they come into practice – that’s where the problem arises. Tax reforms take away too much, young people lose motivation and leave. We have potential, but the system suffocates them.
Interviewer:
Who are your key partners?
Interviewee:
Mostly our subcontractors. They make semi-finished products for us. This cooperation is purely economic – it’s not about sustainable networking.
Interviewer:
How do you build trust with partners?
Interviewee:
Through years and real meetings. Today, Zoom and similar tools are used too much. I don’t like that – personal contact is important.
Interviewer:
What about general cooperation between companies – do you have an opinion?
Interviewee:
I don’t have experience in this area, so I’d rather not comment.
Interviewer:
How do you assess the level of trust in symbiotic networks?
Interviewee:
I think it’s quite high.
Interviewer:
Do you have a suggestion for improving processes in these networks?
Interviewee:
That people in leading positions finally face reality. They shouldn’t just sit in offices and write whatever comes to mind – they should go out, visit companies, and use common sense.
Interviewer:
And finally – do you think your company could still improve its efficiency?
Interviewee:
Of course. There are always possibilities for improvement.
