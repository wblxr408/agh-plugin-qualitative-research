Organization: 7
Interviewer:
Thank you very much for taking the time to talk with us. First, I’d like to know – how is your company structured?
Interviewee:
Our company is family-owned, and we are engaged in the production of wrought-iron fences. Everything happens in-house – from design, to production, to installation. We’ve been operating for more than 25 years and have quite a lot of experience. We also have external partners who supply us with materials. Our business model is based on an individual approach and handcrafting – this means each product is unique, as well as very durable.
Interviewer:
Do you think you would be willing to participate in an industrial symbiosis network, where companies would share resources or costs?
Interviewee:
In principle, yes, we are open. But it would be necessary to carefully consider which resources would actually make sense to share and what impact that would have on our costs and work process.
Interviewer:
What about your sustainability goals – do you already have any in place, are you involved in any local collaborations?
Interviewee:
Currently, we are not involved in any sector-specific collaborations. But of course, we must comply with legislation regarding waste management, emissions, material use – iron, paints, and so on, which have an impact on the environment.
Interviewer:
And your company’s location – what does it allow you?
Interviewee:
Slovenia has good infrastructure and access to quality raw materials. It is still possible to operate cost-effectively, especially in craftsmanship. Our location is favorable, as it allows access to local and regional clients.
Interviewer:
How do you assess legislation regarding recycling and waste management?
Interviewee:
The legislation is quite strict and clear, but for small companies it can be demanding – you can’t follow everything. Otherwise, we generate very little waste. Metal is well recyclable and we try to discard as little as possible. We maximize material usage.
Interviewer:
What do you think makes sustainability projects successful?
Interviewee:
I think primarily financial benefit. If subsidies were available – without too much bureaucracy – probably more companies would engage in these activities.
Interviewer:
How do you see local efforts to reduce emissions, waste, and water usage?
Interviewee:
At the local level, I don’t see many initiatives, at least not in our sector. Companies mainly look at costs. Yes, there are subsidies, for example for solar power plants, but procedures are complicated, which discourages small entrepreneurs. The community is more focused on spatial issues, not so much on sustainability.
Interviewer:
What about the attitude of the state and region toward companies that want to work sustainably?
Interviewee:
We don’t notice any systematic approach. More or less, all initiatives start with the companies themselves or with customers looking for sustainable solutions. There are no direct connections or common platforms.
Interviewer:
Do you have experience with material exchanges between companies?
Interviewee:
No, not in our region. But it would make sense to have a platform where companies could share surpluses or materials that someone else could still use.
Interviewer:
What kind of human capital is needed for successful industrial symbiosis?
Interviewee:
You need a combination of technical knowledge, economic analysis, and people who can connect companies and institutions. I think Slovenia has such people, but it is not coordinated – everyone works for themselves. Sustainability is still perceived as a cost, not as an opportunity. If it were placed as a key value already in schools and business education, progress would be faster.
Interviewer:
Who do you usually cooperate with and how did this cooperation start?
Interviewee:
Our key partners are raw material suppliers and companies that provide protective coatings. Cooperation has developed gradually, through long-term relationships. We are not active in sustainability networking – in our case, there is little waste, and raw materials are already optimized.
Interviewer:
Trust is important in collaborations. How do you build it?
Interviewee:
Through quality work and consistency. New partners usually start with trial cooperation – if we prove ourselves, they stay. It’s a gradual growth of the relationship.
Interviewer:
What is your opinion on cooperation among SMEs?
Interviewee:
Most cooperate for economic reasons. Cooperation often starts informally, but later becomes formalized – contracts and conditions are necessary for stable business.
Interviewer:
And what about trust between companies in the exchange of raw materials?
Interviewee:
There is trust in existing supply chains. But in material exchanges, you would need standardization and supervision – so that companies trust each other regarding the quality of materials for reuse.
Interviewer:
What could Slovenia improve regarding industrial symbiosis?
Interviewee:
More impulses from the state – and not just funding calls, but also information, education, initiatives where companies could connect. Platforms for material exchange – that would really help.
Interviewer:
Finally – do you see possibilities for improving efficiency in your company?
Interviewee:
Always. But we are small and don’t have the resources to carry out in-depth analyses and optimizations. If there were concrete guidelines or expert support available, it would be easier. The potential is there, but we cannot fully exploit it on our own.
Interviewer:
Thank you very much for your time and for sharing your experiences.
