Organization: 18
Interviewer: Thank you for taking the time to participate in the project “Strengthening the Development of Industrial Symbiosis Networks in Slovenia – Transition to a Circular Economy”. We’ll discuss topics related to the circular economy, industrial symbiosis, and your experiences. The recording is only for analysis. If you’re ready, we can begin.
Interviewee: Yes, let’s start.
Interviewer: Could you describe your organization – its structure, staff, and activities?
Interviewee: Sure. We employ about 210 people and around 20 students. Roughly half – about 110 – work in software development. Around 80 focus on hardware sales and related services. Then we have support functions – finance, HR, reception – about 20 people.
Interviewer: Would you join an industrial symbiosis network where companies share investments and benefits?
Interviewee: Yes, absolutely, if the economic and environmental benefits were clear. Challenges are aligning interests, fair distribution of costs/benefits, and long-term stability. Differences in business models, legislation, and above all trust can be obstacles.
Interviewer: What about sustainability goals in Slovenia and your region? Do you participate in local initiatives?
Interviewee: As far as I know, there aren’t really formalized networks around us. Of course, we must follow Slovenian and EU sustainability regulations.
Interviewer: What are the advantages of your location in Slovenia? How supportive is the community?
Interviewee: Good transport connectivity, access to skilled labor, supportive business environment. Proximity to markets and suppliers lowers costs. Community support feels weaker than in the past – maybe just my impression, but it seemed more attentive before.
Interviewer: How do you evaluate the legal framework for recycling and reuse? Helpful or restrictive?
Interviewee: The law follows EU rules and promotes recycling, which is good. Companies must separate waste, meet recycling targets, and manage their waste. In practice, enforcement and implementation are weak. Better financial incentives, easier access to secondary raw materials, and stricter penalties would help.
Interviewer: What are the key factors for successful sustainability projects – economic or environmental?
Interviewee: Both matter, but economics usually decides. Financial feasibility, subsidies, technological maturity, and willingness to change are crucial. Readiness itself is often the biggest barrier.
Interviewer: How do you assess Slovenia’s efforts to reduce CO₂ emissions, water use, and waste?
Interviewee: It’s mostly top-down – politics sets rules, then administration, then companies. Individuals decide how much to really act. Honestly – more could be done.
Interviewer: What about the relationship between Slovenia (or local communities) and companies implementing sustainability measures? Any knowledge exchange?
Interviewee: The relationships are basically okay, but could be much stronger. Most companies don’t focus on this – it’s not their primary goal. Without systemic support, progress is slow.
Interviewer: Do companies exchange materials or inputs?
Interviewee: Not in our company. And I don’t really see it much more broadly.
Interviewer: What human capital is needed for successful symbiosis? Does Slovenia have it?
Interviewee: Technical skills, innovation, collaboration, sustainability mindset. Also project management, legal, and financial knowledge. Slovenia doesn’t lack expertise, but it lacks connection – interdisciplinary cooperation and knowledge transfer to practice. More training and support programs would help.
Interviewer: Who are your key partners? Any sustainability exchange with them?
Interviewee: Mainly our suppliers. They expect us to follow sustainability standards. So the collaboration makes sense – both environmentally and economically.
Interviewer: How important is trust in partnerships?
Interviewee: Extremely. Without trust there’s no communication, no collaboration. We build it with consistency, openness, respect for agreements, and long-term investment in relationships.
Interviewer: What’s your view of cooperation between companies in Slovenia?
Interviewee: Most people don’t pay much attention to it.
Interviewer: How would you rate the level of trust among actors in industrial symbiosis in Slovenia?
Interviewee: Honestly? I don’t know.
Interviewer: Suggestions for improving industrial symbiosis processes?
Interviewee: Companies need a clear presentation of what they can gain – and what is expected. Transparency is key.
Interviewer: Do you believe your company could improve ecological efficiency within industrial symbiosis?
Interviewee: There’s always room for improvement. But companies will only join if they see concrete benefits – current or future. That’s just how it is.
Interviewer: Thank you very much for your answers and your time.
Interviewee: You’re welcome, thank you as well.
