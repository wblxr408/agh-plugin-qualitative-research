Organization: 20
Interviewer: Good morning, thank you for taking the time for this conversation. We’re conducting this interview within the ARIS research project, where we’re interested in how companies in Slovenia cooperate in the field of industrial symbiosis and what experiences you have with circular economy practices. At the moment, we’re doing mapping within a work package to better understand networks, company connections, and levels of cooperation. If it’s okay with you, shall we begin?
Interviewee: Of course, let’s begin.
Interviewer: For orientation – can you briefly present your company? What’s your core activity and how are you organized?
Interviewee: We’re a micro-service company engaged in research and development. Our structure is flexible, mostly project-oriented.
Interviewer: Understood, so quite a specialized environment. Great, thank you.
Interviewer: Now to the substance: are you familiar with networks that deal with circular economy or industrial symbiosis? Maybe you even participate in some?
Interviewee: Yes, we are active in this field. In the environment where we operate, there are both economic and environmental benefits from collaboration.
Interviewer: Excellent, interesting.
Interviewer: Where do you see the main obstacles to building such networks – from your business perspective?
Interviewee: Often it is precisely environmental cooperation that limits industrial symbiosis. Differences in understanding between stakeholders are common.
Interviewer: Yes, that seems like a frequent challenge – especially in understanding the essence of collaboration. Thank you for the honest answer.
Interviewer: More broadly – do you think Slovenia, especially at the local level, is doing enough to implement sustainability goals?
Interviewee: I don’t think so. Legislation often makes it difficult for companies to classify and handle materials that could be reused by another firm. Such regulations often limit collaboration opportunities.
Interviewer: Aha, so we could say systemic flexibility and legislative support are missing.
Interviewee: Exactly.
Interviewer: If we stop at your region – do you think your company has any advantages or disadvantages compared to others?
Interviewee: We’re in the Central Slovenia region. Honestly, I don’t see major differences – either positive or negative.
Interviewer: Okay, thank you. So a regionally equal environment, I understand.
Interviewer: How does the legislative framework for recycling, reuse, etc. affect your work?
Interviewee: Not directly, since we’re a service company. It affects more the companies we collaborate with – especially larger, production-oriented ones.
Interviewer: Very concrete, thank you. Have you noticed that economic factors also influence how legislation is created or implemented?
Interviewee: Absolutely. Socio-economic factors are key. Slovenia currently lacks a comprehensive long-term strategy for the green transition. Legislation is often not consistently prepared – there are dissonances because content experts are not necessarily legislative experts.
Interviewer: That’s a very important observation. Do you have an example?
Interviewee: Yes – for instance the Regulation on the Efficient Use of Energy in Buildings from 2022, which in practice still doesn’t function. We have requirements that in Slovenia simply cannot be implemented right now.
Interviewer: What about efforts to reduce emissions, water use, waste – is enough being done?
Interviewee: No. The 2018 strategy is outdated. We lack an updated approach and a concrete plan.
Interviewer: I agree. We’ve heard that from other interviewees too. Thank you.
Interviewer: Would you say Slovenia has any good practices in knowledge exchange among companies on sustainability?
Interviewee: It’s more formalism than actual knowledge sharing. I’m on the executive board of the Chamber of Commerce’s Environment and Energy section – companies do talk there, but not much concrete exchange of knowledge happens. It’s more about discussing trainings than real content collaboration.
Interviewer: Interesting. I notice the same – a big gap between awareness and real action.
Interviewer: What about human capital? Do we have enough knowledge, competences, experience for industrial symbiosis?
Interviewee: The knowledge exists, but transfer is weak. Connections are weak. Without informal ties and cooperation, it will be hard to build real networks.
Interviewer: Looking ahead – where do you see opportunities to start or strengthen sustainable networking?
Interviewee: Industry doesn’t like “projects for projects’ sake.” It needs to see added value. Collaborations must be systemic, externally managed, with clear governance. A digital platform alone isn’t enough – we need organizational support, information support, and active assistance to companies at the start. Many are not ready yet; some don’t even understand the benefits – so they need outside encouragement.
Interviewer: How do you see trust in these relationships? Do you think there’s enough?
Interviewee: I don’t think so – at least not yet. I hope “not yet.” If there isn’t, then we need to seriously ask ourselves what we’re doing.
Interviewer: I completely agree. Without trust, nothing works.
Interviewer: And finally – do you think your organization could be more efficient if matters were better arranged at the national level?
Interviewee: Absolutely. We’ve been working on energy efficiency and renewable energy for over ten years. We see ourselves as promoters of sustainability. We want to continue encouraging others towards greater connectedness and industrial symbiosis.
Interviewer: Excellent, thank you very much. I truly value your opinion and your willingness to share. Your answers were very informative and valuable for our further work.
Interviewee: Thank you as well. All the best with your research.
Interviewer: Goodbye and have a nice day!
Interviewee: Goodbye.
