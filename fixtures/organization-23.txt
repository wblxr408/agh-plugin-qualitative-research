So, how is your organization actually structured?

The activity holder and two employees. It is a family business in the form of a sole proprietorship, operating in the field of tourism. We have a ranch with various animals – alpacas, goats, horses, piglets, chickens, and ducks. Our services are farm tours, yoga and meditation among alpacas, a campsite, workshops, teambuildings, themed events, and product sales. Roles are distributed horizontally, and we divide tasks according to areas of work.
Interviewer:
Very interesting! Have you already tried to establish some forms of cooperation, for example in the direction of industrial symbiosis?
Interviewee:
Yes, we have tried to connect with others in the area. For example, we arranged regular removal of animal manure, which is then used for fertilizing gardens or fields. Also, the wool from alpaca shearing is used in workshops. We try to waste as little as possible.
Interviewer:
Excellent. Do you think there are enough incentives for such cooperation in your environment?
Interviewee:
In some places yes, in some places no. At the municipal level, we have calls and funding that encourage tourism in border areas, but often small sole proprietorships do not meet the conditions. Tourist Information Centers and associations do encourage obtaining local certificates and sustainable practices.
Interviewer:
And what about the location itself – does it help you or limit you?
Interviewee:
We are on the border with Croatia, which means accessibility to foreign tourists and proximity to spas. However, the local community in tourism is still quite passive. Mainly the municipal tourist association is active.
Interviewer:
How do you view legislation regarding recycling and reuse?
Interviewee:
Formal agreements make cooperation difficult. With small partners, we work on the basis of trust and oral agreements, which currently functions. But if we want to expand, we will have to change the strategy.
Interviewer:
Which factors do you see as key to the success of sustainable projects?
Interviewee:
Vision, coordination, legislation (we are in Kozjansko Park, which is restrictive), access to land, financial resources, and limited eligibility conditions for tenders.
Interviewer:
And what about practice in your area regarding CO2, water, and waste?
Interviewee:
Waste management is well organized. Regarding water and CO2, there are shortcomings – public transport is lacking, and some still do not have regulated wastewater.
Interviewer:
Have you had experiences with knowledge exchange and cooperation in the field of sustainability?
Interviewee:
Yes, at the municipal level there are various workshops and trainings. Also at the national level, but we often find out about them by chance, as there is no centralized notification system.
Interviewer:
How would you describe the human capital required for cooperation in symbiosis?
Interviewee:
Soft skills, emotional intelligence, digital competences, organizational abilities, financial literacy, a broad understanding of the market are needed. Networking, understanding legislation, and quick learning are important.
Interviewer:
So, human capital exists, but it is fragmented and insufficiently connected?
Interviewee:
Exactly. There is a lack of a local intermediary role between the national level and local providers. More organized networking would help.
Interviewer:
How did you start cooperation? Did you establish them yourselves?
Interviewee:
Yes. We organized introductory events, invited local actors, communicated personally, sent official offers. Most cooperation originated from personal contact.
Interviewer:
How important is trust in these relations?
Interviewee:
Very. Without it, it doesn’t work. We trust our partners, respect agreements, and also offer free services in exchange for loyalty. Poor relations can be a big problem, as replacements are not easy.
Interviewer:
What do you think about formal and informal cooperation?
Interviewee:
In Slovenia, a lot takes place informally. Acquaintances help things get settled quickly. Bureaucracy in formal cooperation is often an obstacle, so smaller providers tend to avoid it.
Interviewer:
How high is the level of trust in symbiotic networks?
Interviewee:
High. At least in our case. Since it is mutually beneficial, there is no reason for distrust.
Interviewer:
And suggestions for improving such networks?
Interviewee:
More organized events, networking, municipal support, the local community should be a connector between places, regions, and the state.
Interviewer:
Do you think your company can still improve efficiency?
Interviewee:
Of course. We could do more with water (collectors, filters), energy efficiency (solar panels), wool recycling, self-sufficiency with vegetables. There is always room for improvement.
